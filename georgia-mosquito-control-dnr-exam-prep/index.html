Adulticides & ULV

Before starting a neighborhood adulticide route, the applicator should check:

  1. A. Label rate, weather, and notification rules
  2. B. The truck's maintenance schedule for the month
  3. C. Whether residents requested extra-strong spraying
  4. D. That the tank is filled to maximum capacity
Correct answer: A. Label rate, weather, and notification rules
Pre-route checklist: confirm the labeled rate, verify wind and weather are within label limits, and complete any required public notifications. Customer requests for stronger spraying never override the label.
Larvicides

For a large flooded woodland pool full of early-instar larvae, the most practical larvicide is usually:

  1. A. Briquets carefully hand-placed one by one
  2. B. A surface film carefully applied by hand
  3. C. Liquid or granular Bti via ground or air
  4. D. A truck-mounted adulticide fog at dusk
Correct answer: C. Liquid or granular Bti via ground or air
Large, temporary habitats with young larvae are ideal for broadcast Bti — fast, effective, and selective.
Mosquito Biology

The Asian tiger mosquito (Aedes albopictus) is notable because it:

  1. A. Prefers birds and rarely bites people
  2. B. Feeds most actively at dawn and dusk, like Culex species
  3. C. Breeds only in salt marshes and brackish pools
  4. D. Is an aggressive daytime biter, active through the day
Correct answer: D. Is an aggressive daytime biter, active through the day
Unlike the dusk-feeding Culex, the Asian tiger mosquito bites aggressively during daylight — which is why daytime complaints usually point to container-breeding Aedes near the home. Why not the others: it breeds in small artificial containers, not salt marshes; it feeds readily on people, not mainly birds; its peak is daytime, not dawn and dusk.
Regulations & Permits

Why does Georgia require nuisance wildlife control operators to file annual activity reports with DNR?

  1. A. It lets the state track take and removal activity for wildlife management decisions
  2. B. It’s used only to calculate the operator’s annual tax liability each and every year
  3. C. It has no real regulatory purpose — it’s purely historical paperwork kept on file
  4. D. It’s required only in the operator’s first year of licensure and never again after that
Correct answer: A. It lets the state track take and removal activity for wildlife management decisions
Annual reporting gives DNR visibility into statewide nuisance wildlife activity, supporting wildlife management decisions and regulatory oversight of the permit program.
Adulticides & ULV

A ULV sprayer is calibrated at a flow rate of 3 fluid ounces per minute, driven at 10 mph with a 300-foot swath. The application rate is:

  1. A. 0.5 fluid ounces per acre
  2. B. 1.50 fluid ounces per acre
  3. C. 5 fluid ounces per acre
  4. D. 15 fluid ounces per acre
Correct answer: A. 0.5 fluid ounces per acre
Step 1: flow × 495 — 3 × 495 = 1,485. Step 2: speed × swath — 10 × 300 = 3,000. Step 3: divide: 1,485 ÷ 3,000 ≈ 0.5 fl oz per acre. (The 495 is just the conversion that turns mph × feet of swath into acres covered per minute.)
Procedures & Damage ID

What do chipmunk burrows look like?

  1. A. Large mounds of excavated soil piled around the openings
  2. B. Mud shelter tubes climbing the foundation wall
  3. C. Gnawed holes chewed at the roofline and eaves
  4. D. Small clean holes about 2 inches wide, no soil mound
Correct answer: D. Small clean holes about 2 inches wide, no soil mound
Chipmunk burrows are small (about 2 inches), clean, with no excavated soil mound — unlike mole mounds or rat burrows. They often appear along foundations, stone walls, and garden edges.
Regulations & Permits

In Georgia wildlife regulations, what does 'take' mean?

  1. A. Only the deliberate act of killing a wild animal directly
  2. B. Pursuing, hunting, trapping, capturing, killing — or attempting to
  3. C. Only selling an animal’s pelt for commercial profit to a licensed dealer
  4. D. Photographing wildlife at close range out in the wild
Correct answer: B. Pursuing, hunting, trapping, capturing, killing — or attempting to
In wildlife law, 'take' is broad: pursuing, hunting, trapping, capturing, killing, or attempting any of these. Nuisance control work is a form of take — which is why the permit is required.
Procedures & Damage ID

A lawn is pocked with cone-shaped holes dug overnight, and a musky odor lingers. What is the diagnosis?

  1. A. Mole surface ridges crossing the entire lawn
  2. B. Rat burrows dug along the foundation wall
  3. C. Skunks foraging for grubs overnight in the lawn
  4. D. Carpenter ant frass piled below the wood siding outside
Correct answer: C. Skunks foraging for grubs overnight in the lawn
Skunks dig cone-shaped holes in lawns hunting grubs — a classic sign. They are nocturnal, and the unmistakable musk confirms the diagnosis. Grub control plus exclusion resolves most skunk jobs.
Regulations & Permits

Nuisance wildlife control operators are generally required to submit what to DNR on a recurring basis?

  1. A. A weekly inspection log to the client
  2. B. A monthly tax filing
  3. C. An annual activity/harvest report
  4. D. Nothing — no reporting is required
Correct answer: C. An annual activity/harvest report
Permit holders are required to file annual reports of their nuisance wildlife control activity with DNR.
Procedures & Damage ID

A lawn has raised tunnel ridges. How does a technician distinguish mole damage from vole damage?

  1. A. They are the same animal observed at different ages of growth
  2. B. Raised ridges = moles; surface runways with gnawing = voles
  3. C. Moles make surface runways while voles push up ridges
  4. D. There is no reliable way to tell the two apart
Correct answer: B. Raised ridges = moles; surface runways with gnawing = voles
Moles push up surface ridges and volcano-shaped mounds as they tunnel for grubs and earthworms; voles make surface runways and gnaw bark and bulbs. Ridges = moles; runways + gnawing = voles.

Common questions

What score passes the Georgia DNR nuisance wildlife exam?

80% &mdash; higher than Georgia's other pest control exams. PestPass grades DNR practice at the same 80% mark.

Does Florida have an equivalent of the Georgia DNR permit?

No. Florida does not license nuisance wildlife trappers through an equivalent exam; Florida's only exam-based wildlife credential is FDACS's narrow Limited Certification for nonchemical trapping of commensal rodents. The DNR track in PestPass is Georgia-only.
