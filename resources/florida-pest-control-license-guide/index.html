Florida Pest Control License Guide (Chapter 482)

Florida licenses pest control under Chapter 482, Florida Statutes, and Rule 5E-14, administered by FDACS. This guide explains the Certified Operator exam, the 45-job experience rule, ID Cardholders, fumigation Special ID and Limited Certification in plain English.

Florida's pest control licensing system runs on Chapter 482, Florida Statutes, with the detailed rules in Rule 5E-14, administered by FDACS. It is a different system from Georgia's, with its own exam fees, a higher pass mark for operators, and an experience requirement you have to complete before you can even sit for some exams. If you are moving into Florida work, or starting out there, the biggest mistake is assuming the process works like another state's. It does not. This guide walks through each Florida credential, what it costs, and what you must do first. Certified Operator: $300 exam, 75% to pass

The Certified Operator is Florida's operator-level credential under Chapter 482. The exam fee is $300 and the pass mark is 75% — higher than Georgia's 70%, which is worth knowing if you have studied for another state's test and assume the bar is the same. Operator certification is by category, and the categories include General Household Pest Control, Lawn & Ornamental, Termite/WDO and Fumigation. Each category you want has to be earned, and each one carries the experience requirement described next. The Florida Certified Operator exam prep page has sample questions for the operator categories.

The 45-job rule comes before the exam

Under Rule 5E-14.117, an applicant for General Household Pest Control, Termite/WDO or Lawn & Ornamental certification must first participate in a minimum of 45 jobs in Florida under the supervision of a certified operator — in each category sought. Those jobs are documented on Form FDACS-13653. Read that again if you are planning a timeline: the 45 jobs are per category, not total. A technician who wants two categories needs 45 qualifying jobs in each one. This is the step that catches people out, because it means certification is not something you can decide on and test for in the same month. The experience has to be built on real jobs, under a certified operator, and documented as you go. If you are a technician aiming at certification, start the paperwork conversation with your operator early, keep your job documentation clean, and use the waiting time to study — the exam will be there when your 45 jobs are done.

ID Cardholders: training-based, no exam

Not every Florida pest control worker takes an exam. Under s.482.091, ID Cardholders work under a certified operator, and that credential is training-based — it has no exam. This matters in both directions. If you are an ID Cardholder, you do not need to fear an operator-style test, but you do need your training to be solid, because your day-to-day work is what the credential rests on. And if you are studying for the Certified Operator exam, do not let anyone tell you the ID Cardholder track is a shortcut to the same place: they are different credentials with different responsibilities. Fumigation needs a Special ID

Fumigation sits on top of the regular structure. It requires a Special ID, with a $200 exam fee, a $100 renewal and a $25 late fee. Fumigation is also treated differently on the business side: the insurance minimums for fumigation work are $1M/$2M, well above the standard limits below. If fumigation is part of your plans, treat it as its own project — its own credential, its own exam and its own costs. The Florida Fumigation Special ID exam page covers that credential's material.

Limited Certification under s.482.157

Limited Certification, under s.482.157, is Florida's narrower credential family, with a $150 exam fee. One Limited category covers the nonchemical trapping of commensal rodents — a focused scope, not general pest control. That narrowness is the point: Limited Certification exists for specific kinds of work that do not call for a full operator credential, and the exam matches the scope. The Florida specialty pest control exams page covers Limited Certification alongside Florida's L&O and mosquito material.

Insurance and records: the business-side numbers

Two sets of numbers govern the business side. First, insurance: under s.482.071, the minimums are $250k/$500k for bodily injury and $250k/$500k for property damage, with fumigation at $1M/$2M. Second, records: keep records for 2 years for general and fumigation work, and 3 years for new-construction pretreatments. These are exactly the kinds of figures the operator exam loves to test, because they are specific, they differ by work type, and guessing gets them wrong. When you study Florida Regulations, drill the numbers as numbers — fee, limit, years — until each one is attached to the right rule in your head.

Mosquito control is a separate license

One last separation to keep straight: mosquito control licensing in Florida is not part of Chapter 482 operator certification. It sits under Chapter 388, with its own exam and a 70% pass mark. If mosquito work is your goal, that is the track to study — not the GHP category. Common questions

How much is the Florida Certified Operator exam and what score passes?

The exam fee is $300 and the pass mark is 75%. Certification is by category, including General Household Pest Control, Lawn & Ornamental, Termite/WDO and Fumigation.

What is Florida's 45-job rule?

Under Rule 5E-14.117, applicants for GHP, Termite/WDO or L&O certification must first participate in at least 45 jobs in Florida under a certified operator's supervision in each category sought, documented on Form FDACS-13653.

Do Florida ID Cardholders take an exam?

No. Under s.482.091, ID Cardholders work under a certified operator and the credential is training-based, with no exam.

What does the fumigation Special ID cost?

The Special ID exam fee is $200, renewal is $100 and the late fee is $25.

How long must Florida pest control records be kept?

Two years for general and fumigation work, and three years for new-construction pretreatments.
