Georgia Pest Control License Requirements

Georgia has more than one pest control credential, and each one has its own exam, fee and pass mark. This guide lays out the Certified Operator, Registered Technician, contractor license and Category 24 requirements in plain English so you know exactly which test you are studying for.

Georgia pest control licensing can look confusing at first because the state separates the person who takes the operator exam, the registered employee who works under supervision, the business contractor license, and the separate Category 24 applicator exam for lawn and ornamental work. Those are not interchangeable. The first step is matching your job to the right credential; the second is studying for that exam only, at the level it actually tests. Certified Operator: the top Georgia credential

The Certified Operator exam is the main operator-level test for structural pest control in Georgia. The exam fee is $45 and the pass mark is 70%. The exam is built from four areas: General Knowledge, Household Pest Control (HPC), Wood-Destroying Organisms (WDO) and Georgia Regulations. That mix matters for studying. A strong operator candidate needs insect identification and biology, pesticide safety and label knowledge, application methods, wood-destroying organism inspections, and the Georgia rules that govern the work. Truman's 7th edition is the officially recommended study text for this exam, and it is a good backbone for the science side. Practice questions then show you how that knowledge gets asked under exam conditions. You can work through operator-level samples on the Georgia Certified Operator practice test page.

The retake rule is the part to plan around. Under Rule 620-3-.01, amended effective April 8, 2026, after two certification exam failures within 12 months, six months must elapse since the last exam before you can retake it. In plain terms: two misses in a year does not just cost you another fee, it can cost you half a year. That is a strong argument for treating the first attempt like the real deadline — study the regulations and WDO material as seriously as the pest ID, because the exam covers all of it.

Registered Technician: the registered employee exam

The Registered Technician credential — the registered employee — is the entry-level Georgia exam for technicians. The exam fee is $45 and the pass mark is 70%. Before the exam, the training requirement is 10 hours of classroom training plus 70 hours of on-the-job training. The registered employee test is not the operator test with a different name; it is pitched at the technician level, so your study time should focus on the fundamentals a working tech uses every day: basic pest identification, safety, labels and the core rules of the job. The Georgia Registered Technician exam page has sample questions at that level.

The retake rule is also different, and shorter. After a second failure on the registration exam, 30 calendar days must pass before retaking. Use those 30 days on the categories you missed instead of re-reading everything — but it is still a delay worth avoiding by preparing properly the first time.

The Pesticide Contractor License

Separate from the individual exams, Georgia's Pesticide Contractor License is the business-side license, listed at $55 per year in GDA's Pest Control FAQ. Keep it in its own mental box: the contractor license is about the business being licensed, while the Certified Operator and Registered Technician exams are about the individual passing a test. New technicians sometimes hear the $55 figure and assume it is an exam fee. It is not — both individual exams above are $45.

Category 24: Lawn & Ornamental is its own exam

Lawn & Ornamental in Georgia is Category 24, and it is a separate applicator exam — it is not part of the Certified Operator exam. If your work is turf and ornamentals, this is your test. The exam fee is $45 and the pass mark is 70%. The license itself is $90 for 5 years, renewal is $25, and recertification takes 10 hours per 5 years. Records must be kept for 2 years. Because it is a separate exam with its own subject matter — turf pests, ornamental pests, fertilizer and application rules — it deserves its own study track rather than leftover time from operator studying. The Georgia Lawn & Ornamental Category 24 practice test page covers that track.

The five WDO report organisms

For WDO work, Georgia's official inspection report covers five wood-destroying organisms under Rule 620-6-.03. Learn this list exactly as written, because exam questions test it directly:

  • Subterranean termites
  • Powder post beetles
  • Wood boring beetles
  • Drywood termites
  • Wood decaying fungi

Notice that wood boring beetles are their own entry on the list. Questions that swap in a similar-sounding pest, or split one entry into two, are a common trap — the list above is the whole answer.

How to prepare

Start by confirming which credential you actually need, then study that track's categories and nothing else. For the operator exam, pair Truman's 7th edition with operator-level practice questions and pay extra attention to Georgia Regulations and the WDO organism list. For the registered employee exam, master the fundamentals and the training-based knowledge a technician uses daily. For Category 24, study turf and ornamental material on its own schedule. Common questions

How much is the Georgia Certified Operator exam and what score passes?

The exam fee is $45 and the pass mark is 70%. The exam covers General Knowledge, HPC, WDO and Georgia Regulations.

What happens if I fail the Georgia certification exam twice?

Under Rule 620-3-.01 (amended effective April 8, 2026), after two certification failures within 12 months, six months must elapse since the last exam before you can retake it.

What training does a Georgia Registered Technician need?

10 hours of classroom training plus 70 hours of on-the-job training. The exam fee is $45, the pass mark is 70%, and after a second failure you must wait 30 calendar days to retake.

Is Lawn & Ornamental part of the Georgia operator exam?

No. Category 24 Lawn & Ornamental is a separate Georgia applicator exam: $45 exam fee, 70% pass mark, $90 license for 5 years, $25 renewal and 10 recertification hours per 5 years.

Which organisms are on Georgia's official WDO inspection report?

Five, under Rule 620-6-.03: subterranean termites, powder post beetles, wood boring beetles, drywood termites and wood decaying fungi.
