Texas Pest Control License Guide (TDA SPCS)

Texas licenses structural pest control under Occupations Code Chapter 1951 and the SPCS rules in 4 TAC Chapter 7, administered by the Texas Department of Agriculture. This guide explains the business license, Certified Applicator and Technician exams, fees, insurance and renewals in plain English.

Texas runs its own licensing system, separate from every other state's: the Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS), a program of the Texas Department of Agriculture, licenses businesses and the people who work for them under Occupations Code Chapter 1951, with the working rules in Title 4, Chapter 7 of the Texas Administrative Code. If you are starting out in Texas pest control — or moving your license work there — the biggest mistake is assuming the process works like Georgia's or Florida's. It does not. Seven categories under §7.124

Texas issues structural pest control licenses in seven categories: (1) Pest Control — the general household pests in and around structures; (2) Termite and Wood Destroying Insect Control; (3) Lawn and Ornamental; (4) Weed Control; (5) Structural Fumigation; (6) Commodity Fumigation; and (7) Wood Preservation. You hold a license category by category: each one has its own exam, and a business can only offer work in categories where at least one of its certified applicators is licensed. Certified Applicator: $64 exam, 70% to pass

You cannot simply book an exam. TDA must pre-qualify you first: complete the application, pay the license fees to TDA, and wait for the Examination Eligibility Letter. Only then do you register with Metro Institute — the testing company that has administered SPCS exams at more than 31 Texas sites since May 19, 2025 — pay the $64 per-category exam fee, and schedule. Exams are given in English only, you must show photo identification issued by the Texas Department of Public Safety (or an equivalent from another state), and calculators are the only electronic devices allowed. A grade of 70% or better passes, scores are valid for 12 months, and Metro's proctors provide final scores on site.

The apprentice-to-technician pathway

Most technicians start as registered apprentices: employees engaged in sales or service who have not yet passed a technician exam in any category. An apprentice is registered with the department and may work only for the business that registered them. Moving up means completing the required training in a category — including 8 hours of classroom training and 40 hours of on-the-job training recorded on the verified training record — and then passing that category's technician exam. A technician is licensed for each business they work for and may hold multiple categories.

Insurance: $500,000 / $1,000,000

Before a business license (or a certified noncommercial applicator license) is issued, the applicant must show liability insurance of at least $500,000 for bodily injury and property damage per occurrence, with a minimum total annual aggregate of $1,000,000 (4 TAC §7.123). The policy must give the department at least 30 days' notice of cancellation or material change, and if claims pull the aggregate below $1,000,000 during the term, the coverage has to be restored. Older copies of the statute show lower $200,000/$300,000 figures — the current rule's $500,000/$1,000,000 minimums are what TDA actually requires.

Renewal and continuing education

Commercial technician and certified applicator licenses expire annually, on the expiration date of the business license. Renewing late costs 1½ times the renewal fee within 90 days of expiration and twice the fee after that; let a license lapse more than 365 days and you must re-examine. Certified applicators renew with continuing education earned in the preceding calendar year: two CEUs in general training — at least one of them in federal and state laws, pesticide safety, environmental protection, or integrated pest management — plus one CEU in each category held. A certified applicator license will not be renewed with CEUs unmet. Licensees must also notify the department of any change of address, business location, or phone number within 10 business days.

The paperwork customers see

Texas builds consumer protection into the job paperwork. Customers receive a consumer information sheet, and planned indoor treatments at places like multi-unit housing, workplaces, schools, hospitals and day-care centers require written notification at least 48 hours ahead. Termite work carries its own disclosure and reporting rules (§§7.172–7.176), including the state-prescribed wood-destroying-insect report for real-estate transactions. Common questions

Who licenses pest control in Texas?

The Structural Pest Control Service (SPCS) of the Texas Department of Agriculture, under Occupations Code Chapter 1951 and 4 TAC Chapter 7. Agricultural pest control under a different license family is a separate system.

How much does the Texas pest control exam cost?

$64 per category exam, paid to Metro Institute after TDA pre-qualifies you. The license fees themselves — $125 for a Certified Applicator or Technician license, $300 for a business license — are paid to TDA.

What score passes?

70% in each category, for both Certified Applicator and Technician exams.
