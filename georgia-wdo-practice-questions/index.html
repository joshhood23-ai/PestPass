What does the WDO category cover?

Georgia's official WDO inspection report covers exactly five organisms under Rule 620-6-.03: subterranean termites, powder post beetles, wood boring beetles, drywood termites, and wood decaying fungi. The exam tests identification, evidence (mud tubes, frass, exit holes, damage), conducive conditions and inspection procedure.

What is the FIRST step in treating a carpenter ant infestation?

  1. A. Locate nests and identify the moisture source enabling nesting
  2. B. Apply granular bait broadly throughout the entire yard
  3. C. Apply a broadcast spray immediately to all exposed wood surfaces
  4. D. Immediately drill and dust every wall void found in the structure
Correct answer: A. Locate nests and identify the moisture source enabling nesting
Carpenter ant IPM starts with finding all nests and identifying the moisture source — fixing moisture without treating leads to reinfestation.

How does the Formosan termite soldier's head differ from the Eastern subterranean termite soldier?

  1. A. Formosan soldiers have a rectangular head and large mandibles; Eastern soldiers have an oval head with a fontanel
  2. B. Formosan: oval/teardrop-shaped head with a fontanel that sprays white latex. Eastern: more rectangular head.
  3. C. Formosan soldiers lack mandibles and defend only by spraying latex; Eastern soldiers rely on mandibles alone
  4. D. Eastern soldiers have the oval, teardrop-shaped head; Formosan soldiers have the rectangular head
Correct answer: B. Formosan: oval/teardrop-shaped head with a fontanel that sprays white latex. Eastern: more rectangular head.
The Formosan soldier's oval/teardrop head with a prominent fontanel that exudes white sticky latex is the key field mark separating it from the Eastern subterranean soldier's more rectangular head. Formosan soldiers do have mandibles, and Eastern soldiers have a small fontanel too — head shape is what distinguishes them, not color or the mere presence of a fontanel.

Which powderpost beetle CAN re-infest old, dried, seasoned structural lumber?

  1. A. Both lyctid and anobiid beetles are equally capable of re-infesting old, dry wood
  2. B. Anobiid (deathwatch) beetles — lyctids are tied to starch-bearing hardwood sapwood, not old dry structural lumber
  3. C. Lyctid beetles, since this species is capable of re-infesting any wood type
  4. D. Old house borers, since this species only infests new, green, unseasoned lumber
Correct answer: B. Anobiid (deathwatch) beetles — lyctids are tied to starch-bearing hardwood sapwood, not old dry structural lumber
Anobiid beetles can re-infest dried, seasoned, unfinished wood — making anobiid damage in old structural lumber potentially active. Lyctids can also re-infest, but only hardwood whose sapwood still holds starch — never the old, dry structural lumber this question asks about.

WDO inspection tools used in the field include:

  1. A. Flashlight, screwdriver/awl for probing, moisture meter, and tapping
  2. B. A thermal camera and borescope only, without any physical probing tools
  3. C. Only visual inspection, since state law permits no other tools
  4. D. Chemical reagent test kits paired with soil sampling equipment
Correct answer: A. Flashlight, screwdriver/awl for probing, moisture meter, and tapping
Standard WDO inspection tools: flashlight, screwdriver or awl (probing for soft spots/hollow galleries), tapping (hollow sound indicates galleries), and moisture meter (identifies elevated moisture). Borescopes are supplemental.

Formosan termite 'carton nest independence' means:

  1. A. Carton nests that only Formosan termites are known to build in the Southeast
  2. B. A colony that has lost all contact with its original primary queen
  3. C. A term for satellite Formosan colonies forming during swarm season
  4. D. A colony with a carton nest that retains moisture without soil contact
Correct answer: D. A colony with a carton nest that retains moisture without soil contact
Mature Formosan colonies can build moisture-retaining carton nests in wall voids, becoming independent of soil. Standard soil barrier treatment doesn't affect the above-ground nest — direct treatment of the nest location is also required.

The Georgia SPCA requires WDO inspection records to be available for review by:

  1. A. The customer only, and only upon a formal written request to the office
  2. B. Any member of the general public who simply requests them
  3. C. The GSPCC only, and only during formal disciplinary hearings
  4. D. The Georgia Department of Agriculture during routine GDA inspections

Which soil type increases the risk of termiticide leaching to groundwater?

  1. A. Sandy or coarse-textured soil with shallow water table near wells
  2. B. Heavy clay soil with poor drainage and very slow water percolation
  3. C. Silty loam soil with moderate, balanced water retention characteristics
  4. D. Rocky soil with solid bedrock sitting close to the surface
Correct answer: A. Sandy or coarse-textured soil with shallow water table near wells
Termiticide leaching risk is highest in sandy/coarse-textured soils with shallow water tables, particularly near water supply wells. Labels include groundwater advisory statements when this risk applies.

A technician applies termiticide at double the labeled rate, reasoning that 'more chemical means better protection.' What is the compliance problem?

  1. A. None — exceeding the labeled rate is permitted when the infestation is severe
  2. B. Above-label application violates FIFRA; the label rate is both floor and ceiling
  3. C. It is only a violation if the customer complains about the higher chemical load
  4. D. Above-label rates are allowed for pre-construction but not post-construction treatments
Correct answer: B. Above-label application violates FIFRA; the label rate is both floor and ceiling
The label rate is both the minimum and the maximum: applying below the labeled rate fails to meet the label minimum, and applying above it is equally a FIFRA violation. More chemical does not mean better protection — it means off-label application with added environmental and liability risk.

Which factor contributes most to fungal decay of structural wood?

  1. A. Unusually low humidity levels around the structure.
  2. B. The specific color of the exterior paint used.
  3. C. Prolonged direct sunlight exposure on the wood.
  4. D. Sustained elevated moisture within the wood itself.
Correct answer: D. Sustained elevated moisture within the wood itself.
Wood-decay fungi require sustained elevated moisture content in the wood to grow and break down cellulose and lignin. Low humidity, sunlight, and paint color do not drive fungal decay the way persistent moisture does.

A technician treats only the visible exterior foundation perimeter after the slab has already been poured. What has been accomplished?

  1. A. At best a vertical barrier — the horizontal barrier under the slab can no longer be applied conventionally
  2. B. Both the horizontal and vertical barriers, since perimeter treatment automatically covers the area under the slab
  3. C. Nothing at all, since post-pour perimeter treatment provides no protection whatsoever
  4. D. A complete pre-construction treatment, since the exterior perimeter is the only area that matters
Correct answer: A. At best a vertical barrier — the horizontal barrier under the slab can no longer be applied conventionally
Treating only the perimeter after the slab is poured provides a vertical barrier at best — the horizontal barrier under the slab must be applied before the pour, and once missed, can only be addressed later through retrofit drilling and injection.
WDO

Only shed wings are found on a windowsill — no bodies. Which wing trait still points to termites rather than ants?

  1. A. Termite wings are equal in size with a clean break line near the base where they detached
  2. B. Termite wings have a thick, leathery texture unlike the papery wings of any ant species
  3. C. Termite wings are always found in groups of eight still joined at the thorax
  4. D. Termite wings are unequal in length and detach in pairs; ant wings are equal and shed singly
Correct answer: A. Termite wings are equal in size with a clean break line near the base where they detached
Termite alates shed both pairs of equal-length wings along a basal fracture suture, leaving clean stubs — so equal-sized wings with a straight break line near the base identify termites even when the bodies are gone. Ant wings are unequal (front pair larger than hind). No insect wing is leathery, and shed wings are never still joined to a thorax.

Which wood-destroying beetle is most associated with re-infesting the SAME piece of wood across multiple beetle generations?

  1. A. Lyctid powderpost beetles exclusively, among all wood-destroying beetle types
  2. B. The old house borer (Hylotrupes bajulus), which re-infests the same wood
  3. C. Carpenter bees, which primarily excavate rather than consume wood
  4. D. Termites, which technically are not beetles at all
Correct answer: B. The old house borer (Hylotrupes bajulus), which re-infests the same wood
Old house borers are notable for their ability to re-infest the same piece of wood across successive generations, allowing damage to continue accumulating in the same structural member over many years without new external infestation sources.

Which statement about the relationship between termite swarm timing and recent rainfall is accurate?

  1. A. Swarming always occurs on a fixed calendar date each year, regardless of weather conditions
  2. B. Termite swarming timing is entirely unrelated to weather conditions of any kind
  3. C. Many subterranean termites swarm on warm days following rainfall, when humidity is elevated
  4. D. Termites only swarm during dry, low-humidity conditions with no recent rainfall at all
Correct answer: C. Many subterranean termites swarm on warm days following rainfall, when humidity is elevated
Warm, humid conditions following rain are a classic trigger for many subterranean termite species’ swarming behavior, which is part of why swarm activity often appears to cluster around rainy periods in spring.
WDO

Which statement about a termite bait station's active ingredient reaching the queen is most accurate regarding colony elimination goals?

  1. A. The toxicant is applied directly into the queen chamber in every bait system
  2. B. Worker termites are biologically incapable of transferring any substance to the queen
  3. C. Colony elimination never actually depends on affecting the queen specifically
  4. D. Elimination needs toxicant spreading via trophallaxis to reach the queen caste
Correct answer: D. Elimination needs toxicant spreading via trophallaxis to reach the queen caste
Bait systems rely on foraging workers spreading the toxicant through trophallaxis until it eventually reaches the queen and other reproductives — true colony elimination depends on this indirect transfer, since the toxicant isn’t applied to the queen directly.

Where is bostrichid frass typically found, and how does that compare to lyctid frass?

  1. A. Loose and flour-like, sifting freely out of the exit holes just like lyctid frass
  2. B. Gritty pellets that always fall in loose piles below the holes, exactly like anobiid frass
  3. C. Packed in the galleries but talcum-fine, with no visible wood fragments
  4. D. Tightly packed in galleries with wood fragments — unlike fine, loose lyctid frass
Correct answer: D. Tightly packed in galleries with wood fragments — unlike fine, loose lyctid frass
Lyctid frass is fine, loose, and falls out of the exit hole readily. Bostrichid frass is coarser, mixed with small wood fragments, and stays packed tightly inside the gallery — the exit hole itself often looks comparatively clean. (Anobiid frass is also gritty, but anobiids favor damp wood and make smaller 1/16–1/8 inch holes.)

Is it accurate to say old house borers only infest old houses, as the common name suggests?

  1. A. Partly — they infest new and old structures equally, regardless of wood age
  2. B. No — they're more common in newer construction, drawn to less-seasoned softwood framing
  3. C. Yes — old house borers exclusively infest structures more than 50 years old, exactly as the name implies
  4. D. No — old house borers attack only hardwoods, so softwood framing age is irrelevant
Correct answer: B. No — they're more common in newer construction, drawn to less-seasoned softwood framing
The common name is misleading — old house borers are actually more frequently found in newer homes, since they’re drawn to less-seasoned softwood framing lumber. They can still be found in older structures too, but "old house" in the name refers to where the species was first commonly noticed, not where it’s most often found.
WDO

Lyctid (true powderpost) beetles infest:

  1. A. Old, decayed pine framing members
  2. B. Pressure-treated exterior deck posts and rails
  3. C. Hardwood sapwood, like new oak flooring
  4. D. Concrete foundation walls and piers
Correct answer: C. Hardwood sapwood, like new oak flooring
True powderpost beetles (Lyctidae) attack only the sapwood of hardwoods with sufficient starch — new oak flooring, trim, and furniture. Their holes are tiny (about 1/16 inch) with flour-like frass.
WDO

How did the deathwatch beetle get its name?

  1. A. Tapping males make a ticking sound once thought to foretell a death
  2. B. Their emergence holes appear only in wood from trees that died standing
  3. C. They are named for the deathwatch stamp beetle, a related stored-product pest
  4. D. Adults play dead (thanatosis) when handled — 'watching for death'
Correct answer: A. Tapping males make a ticking sound once thought to foretell a death
Deathwatch beetles tap their heads against wood to attract mates — a ticking sound heard in quiet old buildings. Superstition held that hearing it foretold a death in the house. (Thanatosis — playing dead — is real beetle behavior, but it's not the origin of this name.)

An old house borer larva is best described as:

  1. A. A tiny hairy larva that feeds only on wool fabric
  2. B. A 1-inch cream-colored grub with three pairs of legs
  3. C. A small C-shaped white grub with no visible legs at all
  4. D. A flat legless borer with a conspicuously wide head
Correct answer: B. A 1-inch cream-colored grub with three pairs of legs
Old house borer larvae are big (up to ~1 in), cream-colored, with three pairs of thoracic legs — much larger than anobiid larvae. Why not the others: C-shaped legless grubs are scarabs; wide flat heads are flatheaded borers; fabric feeders are carpet beetles.
WDO

Which wood-destroying beetle is LEAST likely to re-infest seasoned wood indoors?

  1. A. Old house borer (re-infests seasoned softwood)
  2. B. Deathwatch beetle (re-infests damp softwood)
  3. C. Bark beetle — needs dying trees, never in dry wood
  4. D. True powderpost beetle (re-infests starchy sapwood)
Correct answer: C. Bark beetle — needs dying trees, never in dry wood
Bark beetles need dying/fresh trees and do not colonize dry indoor wood at all. Why not the others: old house borers and anobiids famously re-infest; lyctids re-infest starchy hardwood sapwood.
WDO

Wood-decay fungi and wood-boring beetles are listed as separate WIIR organisms because:

  1. A. Only fungi can cause genuine structural damage to wood
  2. B. The inspection form simply ran out of space for detail
  3. C. Fungi are simply another small kind of insect pest entirely
  4. D. Different kingdoms — different evidence, causes, remedies
Correct answer: D. Different kingdoms — different evidence, causes, remedies
Fungi and beetles differ in biology, evidence (rot vs. holes/frass), moisture relationships, and treatment — the form tracks them separately. Why not the others: fungi are not insects; the form is deliberately specific; beetles absolutely cause structural damage.

Two years after a bait system installation, routine monitoring shows termite hits in stations have declined to zero, but the annual inspection finds fresh mud tubes on a foundation wall 30 feet from the nearest station. What is the correct response?

  1. A. Do nothing, because the bait stations prove the colony is eliminated
  2. B. Spot-treat the active area with liquid termiticide; keep monitoring
  3. C. Immediately fumigate the entire structure for termites
  4. D. Remove the entire bait system as a failure and start over
Correct answer: B. Spot-treat the active area with liquid termiticide; keep monitoring
Fresh mud tubes are evidence of current activity breaching the protection zone, and stations 30 feet away may not intercept every foraging front — this is a breakthrough requiring supplemental treatment at the active site, not abandonment of a system that is otherwise working. Fumigation would not reach a subterranean colony, and removing the system discards two years of colony suppression for one localized failure.

True powderpost beetle exit holes are what size?

  1. A. 1/16 to 1/8 inch in diameter
  2. B. 1/32 to 1/16 inch in diameter
  3. C. 1/4 to 3/8 inch across
  4. D. 1/2 inch in diameter
Correct answer: B. 1/32 to 1/16 inch in diameter
True powderpost beetles (Lyctinae) make the smallest exit holes of the wood-boring beetles — just 1/32 to 1/16 inch, about pinhead size. Holes this tiny in hardwood with fine powdery frass point to lyctids.
Inspection Procedures

A technician taps a sill plate and it sounds hollow, then a screwdriver pushes in easily. The correct next step is:

  1. A. Probe further to map the damage and find the cause
  2. B. Immediately declare the sill destroyed and quote replacement
  3. C. Tell the customer it is normal aging and move along
  4. D. Spray the area with insecticide and leave the site
Correct answer: A. Probe further to map the damage and find the cause
Hollow sound + easy penetration = damaged wood; the inspector maps how far it extends and identifies the pest or moisture cause. Why not the others: quoting replacement is not the inspector’s job; spraying without diagnosis is malpractice; dismissing it misses the finding.

A sill shows damage only where it contacts a concrete porch, with no mud tubes. The likely cause:

  1. A. Moisture decay from concrete contact
  2. B. Drywood termites in the sill plate
  3. C. Carpenter bees boring in the concrete
  4. D. Subterranean termites tunneling upward
Correct answer: A. Moisture decay from concrete contact
Concrete wicks moisture into wood; localized rot at the contact point with no tubes points to decay, not termites. Why not the others: subterranean termites would show tubes; drywood termites need no soil contact but leave pellets; carpenter bees bore round holes.

Personal protective equipment for a crawlspace inspection typically includes:

  1. A. No PPE, since crawlspaces are clean environments
  2. B. Only a basic pair of safety glasses
  3. C. Hard hat, respirator, gloves, and coveralls
  4. D. A full fumigation SCBA breathing setup
Correct answer: C. Hard hat, respirator, gloves, and coveralls
Crawlspaces carry dust, mold spores, fiberglass, and animal waste — coveralls, gloves, and respiratory protection are standard. Why not the others: SCBA is for fumigation, not inspections; “clean” is never assumed; glasses alone are insufficient.

After a treatment, the technician returns for a warranty inspection and finds new mud tubes. The correct action is:

  1. A. Just spray right over the tubes and leave quickly today
  2. B. Ignore them; the warranty already paid out
  3. C. Blame the customer and void all warranty coverage
  4. D. Document it, find the breach, and retreat per warranty
Correct answer: D. Document it, find the breach, and retreat per warranty
New activity under warranty = document, find the breach (disturbed barrier, new contact), and retreat per terms. Why not the others: ignoring or blaming the customer violates the warranty; spraying over tubes without documentation hides the evidence.

A sill plate probe sinks in easily along a 6-foot run but the damage stops at a masonry joint. The inspector should:

  1. A. Recommend tent fumigation for the whole house
  2. B. Report only the first foot of damage
  3. C. Report the full extent and note where it stops
  4. D. Assume the damage continues through the masonry
Correct answer: C. Report the full extent and note where it stops
Report what was actually found — extent mapped, stopping point noted — without inventing hidden damage. Why not the others: assuming through-masonry spread is speculation; underreporting hides findings; fumigation is not the response to localized decay.

A garage door frame shows rot at the bottom where sprinklers hit it daily. The corrective recommendation is:

  1. A. Apply a termiticide trench around the garage
  2. B. Fumigate the entire garage structure
  3. C. Redirect sprinklers and replace rotted wood
  4. D. Install termite bait stations nearby
Correct answer: C. Redirect sprinklers and replace rotted wood
The cause is chronic wetting — fix the water (redirect sprinklers) and address the rotted wood. Why not the others: fumigation and termiticides target insects, not rot; baits are for termites, which are not the finding.
