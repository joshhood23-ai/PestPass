Florida specialty exams: L&O, Limited Certification & Mosquito

Beyond the core operator categories, Florida runs three specialty credentials — and PestPass covers all three. Lawn & Ornamental for turf and ornamental work. Limited Certification under s.482.157, Florida's narrow credential for nonchemical trapping of commensal rodents — the closest thing Florida has to a wildlife credential. And Mosquito Control under Chapter 388 for public-health pest control, the one Florida exam that passes at 70%.

Lawn & Ornamental

The standard inspection method and action threshold for hunting billbug is:

  1. A. Under all conditions, flush a marked 2 feet by 2 feet area with soapy water and count what surfaces; treat when the count reaches 2 to 4 adults per 4 square feet, no matter the circumstances
  2. B. Cut three sides of a one-foot-square piece of sod about 2 inches deep, lay it back, and sift the soil for larvae; an average of ten billbugs per square foot justifies applying an insecticide
  3. C. Float the insects up in a bottomless metal can pushed into the soil and filled with water; treat when counts stay high across four samples
  4. D. Count larvae per square foot of turf with a soap flush and treat when the count reaches three mature larvae per square foot
Correct answer: B. Cut three sides of a one-foot-square piece of sod about 2 inches deep, lay it back, and sift the soil for larvae; an average of ten billbugs per square foot justifies applying an insecticide
To inspect for billbugs, cut three sides of a one-foot-square piece of sod about 2 inches deep, lay it back, and sift the soil for larvae; as a rule of thumb, an average of ten billbugs per square foot justifies applying an insecticide (TP-085). The soap flush figure is the mole cricket threshold and three per square foot is the fall armyworm threshold.
Lawn & Ornamental

How does twospotted spider mite feeding produce the stippled, bleached look on foliage?

  1. A. It excretes honeydew over the leaf, and the fungus growing in it bleaches the tissue underneath
  2. B. It injects a toxic saliva as it feeds, which contorts and stunts the new growth it feeds on, regardless of weather conditions
  3. C. It scrapes away the epidermis of the leaf with rasping-sucking mouthparts as it feeds
  4. D. Each feeding puncture collapses mesophyll cells - roughly 18-22 cells per minute - creating small chlorotic spots that merge
Correct answer: D. Each feeding puncture collapses mesophyll cells - roughly 18-22 cells per minute - creating small chlorotic spots that merge
Twospotted spider mite feeding collapses mesophyll cells (destroying roughly 18-22 cells per minute), so each feeding site becomes a small chlorotic spot; continued feeding produces a stippled-bleached effect, then yellow, gray, or bronze leaves and possible defoliation.
Lawn & Ornamental

Which set of criteria is used to classify weeds for identification and management purposes?

  1. A. Seed color, root depth, and height at maturity, in every case and under all conditions
  2. B. Flower color, leaf texture, and growth rate
  3. C. Morphology (monocot or dicot), structure (broadleaf, grass, or sedge), and life cycle
  4. D. Soil type, shade tolerance, and drought tolerance
Correct answer: C. Morphology (monocot or dicot), structure (broadleaf, grass, or sedge), and life cycle
UF/IFAS classifies weeds by morphology (monocot or dicot), by structure (broadleaf, grass, or sedge/rush), and by life cycle (annual, biennial, or perennial). Correct classification is what lets an operator match the weed to the right control.
Lawn & Ornamental

Dicamba, metsulfuron, and atrazine products should not be applied over the root zones of trees and shrubs because they:

  1. A. Can be absorbed by tree and shrub roots and severely injure them
  2. B. Volatilize and drift onto the foliage within hours
  3. C. Permanently sterilize the soil under the canopy
  4. D. Concentrate in the soil and attract damaging insects, in all cases
Correct answer: A. Can be absorbed by tree and shrub roots and severely injure them
Dicamba, metsulfuron, and atrazine products can be absorbed by the roots of trees and shrubs and severely injure them. For that reason they should not be applied over ornamental root zones.
Limited Certification

For the s.482.156 limited commercial landscape maintenance examination, the statute sets the fee by rule at:

  1. A. Not less than $25 and not more than $75
  2. B. A flat $300 per category
  3. C. Not less than $50 and not more than $150
  4. D. Not less than $100 and not more than $300
Correct answer: C. Not less than $50 and not more than $150
Section 482.156(2) sets the examination fee by department rule at not less than $50 and not more than $150.
Limited Certification

A landscape crew has four workers, and only the crew leader holds an LCLM certificate. Who on the crew may apply pesticides?

  1. A. All four workers, under the leader's certificate and supervision
  2. B. The leader and one trainee designated in writing each day
  3. C. Only the leader — each worker who applies must be individually certified
  4. D. All four workers, if the leader remains on the same property
Correct answer: C. Only the leader — each worker who applies must be individually certified
The LCLM certificate confers no supervision authority: every landscape worker who applies pesticides must be individually certified (or work under a licensed pest control operator) — a crew cannot apply under one member's certificate (s.482.156(4)(c)).
Limited Certification

An LCLM holder lets the certificate expire and does not renew with proof of continuing education within 60 days after expiration. What is required to become certified again?

  1. A. Recertification only after reexamination
  2. B. Payment of the $50 late charge and the $75 renewal fee
  3. C. A new insurance certificate on form FDACS-13688 only
  4. D. A written request for reinstatement to the department
Correct answer: A. Recertification only after reexamination
Failure to renew with CE proof within 60 days after expiration means the holder may be recertified only after reexamination (s.482.156(3)).
Adulticides & ULV

In the CDC bottle bioassay, insecticide resistance is assessed by:

  1. A. Exposing laboratory-reared larvae to treated water samples collected in the field for a full 24 hours
  2. B. Spraying small field plots and counting the dead adults found at dawn
  3. C. Placing field-collected mosquitoes in insecticide-coated bottles and recording mortality over 2 hours
  4. D. Testing blood meals from field mosquitoes for insecticide residues
Correct answer: C. Placing field-collected mosquitoes in insecticide-coated bottles and recording mortality over 2 hours
The CDC bottle bioassay exposes field-collected mosquitoes inside bottles coated with a known insecticide dose; mortality is recorded over a 2-hour period to see whether the wild population still responds as it should.
Larvicides

Some mosquito control programs hold tire amnesty events primarily to:

  1. A. Recycle the rubber into surfacing for public playgrounds
  2. B. Collect tires for use in mosquito research colonies
  3. C. Remove a container habitat that keeps refilling with rainwater
  4. D. Provide residents with free replacement tires for their vehicles
Correct answer: C. Remove a container habitat that keeps refilling with rainwater
Tire amnesty events exist to get a persistent, hard-to-empty mosquito breeding habitat out of the community; the recycling or disposal that follows is secondary to removing the habitat.
Mosquito Biology

What does integrated mosquito management (IMM) mean in practice?

  1. A. Relying on one adulticide product rotated to a new one each season as the sole control method
  2. B. Using multiple control methods that target multiple mosquito life stages
  3. C. Applying larvicides only after adult complaints are received
  4. D. Treating every water body in the district on a fixed calendar
Correct answer: B. Using multiple control methods that target multiple mosquito life stages
IMM combines several control methods aimed at different life stages of the mosquito rather than depending on any single tactic; the American Mosquito Control Association considers it the best overall approach to mosquito management.

Common questions

What is Florida Limited Certification?

A narrow FDACS credential under s.482.157 covering nonchemical control &mdash; trapping &mdash; of commensal rodents. It is the only exam-based wildlife credential in Florida; there is no Florida equivalent of Georgia's DNR nuisance wildlife permit.

What score passes the Florida mosquito control exam?

70%. Mosquito control certification under Chapter 388 (public health pest control) is the exception to Florida's usual 75% pass mark.

How much is the Limited Certification exam?

$150 through FDACS.
