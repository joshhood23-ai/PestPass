  1. A. Identification is only needed for billing records and customer reports; the main tool is a smartphone camera used to document damage, and there is no need to consult published guides, the county extension service, or any insect pest collection.
  2. B. Proper identification gives the PMP access to the body of knowledge on pest biology and life cycle; tools include published guides, the county extension service, and a company insect pest collection for matching specimens.
  3. C. Identification satisfies the label's legal requirement; the only approved tool is a hand lens issued by the state department.
  4. D. Identification helps the customer trust the technician; tools include advertising brochures and the company's social media pages.
Correct answer: B. Proper identification gives the PMP access to the body of knowledge on pest biology and life cycle; tools include published guides, the county extension service, and a company insect pest collection for matching specimens.
IPM Feature 1 is Identification — proper ID gives the PMP access to the body of knowledge on pest biology and life cycle, using published guides, the county extension service, and a company insect pest collection for matching specimens.

Which SDS section would you check first for firefighting guidance during a chemical fire involving a pesticide?

General Knowledge

A technician spills a liquid concentrate on their forearm. The FIRST action is:

  1. A. Cover it with a towel and call a supervisor
  2. B. Finish the current job and wash off once it's complete
  3. C. Apply a neutralizing agent directly onto the affected skin
  4. D. Flush skin with plenty of water for 15-20 minutes
Correct answer: D. Flush skin with plenty of water for 15-20 minutes
Immediate flushing with large amounts of water for at least 15-20 minutes is the universal first aid for skin pesticide exposure. Remove contaminated clothing. Never apply neutralizing agents.
General Knowledge

What is the generally recommended order when tank mixing wettable powders, liquids, and surfactants together?

  1. A. Wettable powders first, then liquids, with surfactants added last in the tank
  2. B. Liquids added first, then surfactants, then wettable powders last in that order
  3. C. Surfactants added first, then wettable powders, then liquids last
  4. D. The order does not matter at all for any tank mix combination
Correct answer: A. Wettable powders first, then liquids, with surfactants added last in the tank
A common recommended order of addition is wettable powders before liquids, with surfactants added last — incorrect order is a common, avoidable cause of tank mixing failures.
General Knowledge

Which site conditions make groundwater contamination by leaching most likely?

  1. A. Heavy clay soil over deep, fractured bedrock formations far below
  2. B. A steep, fully paved and sealed parking lot
  3. C. Deeply frozen ground in the middle of winter
  4. D. Sandy soil over a shallow water table with soluble products
Correct answer: D. Sandy soil over a shallow water table with soluble products
Leaching moves pesticides downward through soil to groundwater. It is worst where water moves easily — coarse sandy soils — and where the water table is shallow, especially with soluble, persistent products.

HPC practice questions

Household Pest Control: ants, cockroaches, stored-product pests, flies, rodents and the day-to-day accounts a Georgia operator runs.

HPC

Which statement about pharaoh ant colony activity patterns indoors is accurate?

  1. A. Indoor pharaoh ant colonies always die off within one year no matter the conditions
  2. B. Pharaoh ants become completely dormant indoors during every single winter month of the year
  3. C. Pharaoh ants can only survive in outdoor nests and never establish fully indoors
  4. D. Pharaoh ant colonies stay active year-round indoors, where climate control removes winter
Correct answer: D. Pharaoh ant colonies stay active year-round indoors, where climate control removes winter
Because pharaoh ants can establish colonies entirely within a climate-controlled structure, they aren’t subject to the seasonal slowdown outdoor ant colonies experience — a heated building can sustain year-round activity regardless of the season outside.
HPC

What distinguishes the rice weevil from the granary weevil?

  1. A. The rice weevil is strictly limited to infesting rice and no other grain type
  2. B. The granary weevil lacks the elongated snout the rice weevil has
  3. C. The rice weevil can fly with wing markings; the granary weevil can't fly
  4. D. The granary weevil is fully aquatic, while the rice weevil is not
Correct answer: C. The rice weevil can fly with wing markings; the granary weevil can't fly
Both are true weevils with an elongated, curved snout, but the rice weevil has functional wings and four pale spots on its wing covers, while the granary weevil is flightless and uniformly dark. Neither species is limited to a single grain type despite the common names.
HPC

Which cockroach has two dark parallel stripes on its pronotum?

  1. A. American cockroach
  2. B. German cockroach, with two dark stripes on the pronotum
  3. C. Oriental cockroach
  4. D. Smokybrown cockroach, a large dark species found in trees and mulch
Correct answer: B. German cockroach, with two dark stripes on the pronotum
The German cockroach is 1/2 inch, tan, with two dark stripes on the pronotum. Most common small indoor cockroach in homes.
HPC

What does finding multiple nymphal instars of German cockroaches indicate?

  1. A. A perfectly normal population level for a lightly infested kitchen
  2. B. An indication that the cockroaches are about to swarm outdoors
  3. C. A recent infestation that was only introduced within the last week
  4. D. An established, multi-generational reproducing roach infestation
Correct answer: D. An established, multi-generational reproducing roach infestation
Multiple nymphal instars (different size groups) indicate ongoing multi-generational breeding — an established, well-entrenched infestation requiring aggressive treatment and close follow-up.
HPC

An inspection finds only adult German cockroaches — no nymphs or oothecae. What does this suggest?

  1. A. An established, multi-generational breeding population is definitely present
  2. B. The infestation has already been completely eliminated by a prior treatment
  3. C. A recent introduction or transient adults, not an established population
  4. D. German cockroaches are reproducing here without any nymphal stages
Correct answer: C. A recent introduction or transient adults, not an established population
Multiple nymphal instars plus oothecae indicate an established breeding population. Adults alone, with no nymphs or egg cases, suggest a recent introduction or transient adults rather than on-site breeding.
HPC

What does a German cockroach ootheca (egg capsule) look like, and how is it carried?

  1. A. A dark, bean-shaped capsule glued into hidden cracks and crevices
  2. B. A frothy tan mass of eggs floating on the surface of standing water outdoors
  3. C. A light-brown purse-shaped capsule carried until just before hatching
  4. D. A papery, lantern-shaped egg case suspended beneath outdoor eaves
Correct answer: C. A light-brown purse-shaped capsule carried until just before hatching
A German cockroach ootheca is a purse-shaped, light-brown capsule holding 30–40 eggs. The female carries it protruding from her abdomen until just before hatch — which is why one female can seed an infestation.

WDO practice questions

Wood-Destroying Organisms: subterranean termites, powderpost beetles, wood-decaying fungi, inspections and the Official Georgia Wood Infestation Inspection Report.

WDO

A technician reports powderpost-type damage with flour-fine, loose frass — but measures the exit holes at 6 millimeters across. What is the correct professional conclusion?

  1. A. Inconsistent evidence — lyctid frass pairs with 1-1.5 mm holes; re-measure
  2. B. Lyctid beetles — flour-like frass is diagnostic regardless of hole size
  3. C. Bostrichid beetles — 6 mm holes are diagnostic regardless of frass texture
  4. D. Old house borer — 6 mm oval holes with fine frass are its signature
Correct answer: A. Inconsistent evidence — lyctid frass pairs with 1-1.5 mm holes; re-measure
Step 1: Convert the hole size: 6 mm ÷ 25.4 = about 0.24 inch, roughly 1/4 inch. Step 2: Compare with lyctid holes: lyctid (true powderpost) exit holes are only 1/32 to 1/16 inch, about 1 to 1.5 mm, so 6 mm holes are far too large. Step 3: Weigh the frass: flour-fine, loose frass points to lyctids, while roughly 6 mm holes with tightly packed coarse frass point to bostrichids. Step 4: When two evidence streams conflict like this, re-check the measurements and observations before committing to an ID — misidentification means mistreatment.
WDO

How long is the typical lyctid (true powderpost) life cycle?

  1. A. About one month from egg to emerging adult
  2. B. About one year under favorable warm conditions
  3. C. About three to five years, like the old house borer
  4. D. Two to ten years depending on wood moisture, like the old house borer
Correct answer: B. About one year under favorable warm conditions
Lyctid powderpost beetles typically complete a generation in about one year in favorable conditions — which is why new flooring can show emergence holes within a year of installation. (The multi-year options describe the old house borer — the classic cross-species confusion.)
WDO

What is the FIRST step in treating a carpenter ant infestation?

  1. A. Locate nests and identify the moisture source enabling nesting
  2. B. Apply granular bait broadly throughout the entire yard
  3. C. Apply a broadcast spray immediately to all exposed wood surfaces
  4. D. Immediately drill and dust every wall void found in the structure
Correct answer: A. Locate nests and identify the moisture source enabling nesting
Carpenter ant IPM starts with finding all nests and identifying the moisture source — fixing moisture without treating leads to reinfestation.
WDO

Formosan termites entering through an interior utility penetration in a slab can be missed because:

  1. A. Standard moisture meters are entirely incapable of detecting any Formosan termite activity
  2. B. Formosan termites are physically too small to detect using any standard inspection tools available
  3. C. Interior penetrations may lack exterior mud tubes, making them invisible to perimeter inspection
  4. D. Formosan termites do not build mud tubes at any location, whether interior or exterior
Correct answer: C. Interior penetrations may lack exterior mud tubes, making them invisible to perimeter inspection
Formosan termites can enter through interior utility penetrations without producing visible exterior mud tubes. A thorough interior inspection including probing accessible floor areas and checking all interior penetrations is essential.
WDO

Which statement about the relationship between wood-to-soil contact and WDO conducive conditions is most accurate?

  1. A. Wood-to-soil contact has no bearing on termite risk if the wood is pressure-treated
  2. B. Wood-to-soil contact gives termites the moisture and a direct entry path they need
  3. C. Conducive conditions only ever refer to interior moisture, never exterior wood placement
  4. D. Wood-to-soil contact is only a concern for drywood, not subterranean termites
Correct answer: B. Wood-to-soil contact gives termites the moisture and a direct entry path they need
Wood-to-soil contact is one of the most significant conducive conditions specifically because it offers subterranean termites both direct access to the structure and the moisture they need, bypassing the need to build mud tubes at all in that specific location.
WDO

A homeowner finds powder sifting from 1/16-inch holes in a new oak cabinet. The most likely culprit:

  1. A. Bark beetles under the cabinet’s finish
  2. B. Deathwatch beetles in old structural timbers
  3. C. True powderpost beetles in hardwood sapwood
  4. D. Old house borers in seasoned pine framing
Correct answer: C. True powderpost beetles in hardwood sapwood
Tiny (1/16 in) holes + fine powder in new hardwood = lyctid true powderpost beetles attacking sapwood starch. Why not the others: deathwatch prefers old damp softwood; old house borers need larger softwood timbers; bark beetles attack dying trees, not finished furniture.

The Georgia Structural Pest Control Act and its rules — licensing, records, contracts, guarantees and the paperwork that keeps an operator legal.

Georgia Regulations

A homeowner calls 120 days after an Official Georgia Wood Infestation Inspection Report was issued, demanding free re-treatment for beetle holes that the report listed as inactive. The company's correct position is:

  1. A. The 90-day guarantee has expired; a new inspection is the proper response
  2. B. The official form carries a lifetime warranty on its findings
  3. C. The homeowner is entitled to treatment any time within one year of the report
  4. D. Inactive findings void the report, so no obligation ever existed
Correct answer: A. The 90-day guarantee has expired; a new inspection is the proper response
The Official Georgia WIIR guarantees its findings for 90 days — at 120 days the company is no longer bound by the original report, and the professional response is a fresh inspection (conditions change; old holes can become active). This is why the report's date matters: the guarantee window is finite, and work beyond it requires new documented findings.
Georgia Regulations

If a licensee provides pesticide use records electronically, the property owner or custodian must:

  1. A. Visit the company's office in person to pick up a printed copy of the records
  2. B. Hold a valid Georgia driver's license as proof of identity for the account
  3. C. Pay a $10 electronic delivery fee before the records can be emailed to them
  4. D. Sign an electronic communication acknowledgement form that the licensee keeps
Correct answer: D. Sign an electronic communication acknowledgement form that the licensee keeps
The signed acknowledgement form is kept for as long as the contract is in effect or for 2 years past the contract's expiration, whichever is applicable.
Georgia Regulations

Which agency enforces the Georgia Structural Pest Control Act?

  1. A. The Department of Labor
  2. B. The Georgia Department of Agriculture
  3. C. The Structural Pest Control Commission alone
  4. D. The federal EPA
Correct answer: B. The Georgia Department of Agriculture
The GDA is the enforcement agency for the SPCA. The GSPCC administers standards and certifications jointly with the GDA.
Georgia Regulations

Georgia recertification requires how many continuing-education hours for HPC, WDO, and Fumigation respectively?

  1. A. 12 hours for each of the three categories
  2. B. 25 hours for each of the three categories
  3. C. 40 hours for HPC, 40 for WDO, and 20 for Fumigation
  4. D. 25 for HPC, 25 for WDO, and 12 for Fumigation
Correct answer: D. 25 for HPC, 25 for WDO, and 12 for Fumigation
Georgia requires 25 hours of approved continuing education for HPC recertification, 25 hours for WDO, and 12 hours for Fumigation. These are set regulatory numbers worth memorizing directly.
Georgia Regulations

Why does Georgia require every issued Official Georgia WIIR — even ones showing no activity — to appear in the company’s monthly records?

  1. A. So inspectors can bill customers twice for a single inspection
  2. B. So the Commission can reconcile issued reports against company records
  3. C. So the Department can publish each inspector’s findings in a public registry
  4. D. So lenders can verify each report directly with the Commission
Correct answer: B. So the Commission can reconcile issued reports against company records
The point is reconciliation: matching issued reports against monthly records lets the Department verify that every inspection was reported and accounted for. Why not the others: findings are not published in a public registry, lenders verify reports with the issuing company, and the rule has nothing to do with billing.
Georgia Regulations

A licensee must notify the Secretary in writing of a change of address, or the opening or closing of an office, within:

  1. A. 30 days
  2. B. 90 days
  3. C. 10 days
  4. D. 48 hours
Correct answer: C. 10 days
Licenses are displayed in a conspicuous place in the licensed office, with the duplicate license in any sub-office.

What does FIFRA stand for, and what does it govern?

  1. A. The Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act; it governs the transport of hazardous waste across state lines
  2. B. The Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act; it governs the registration, sale and use of pesticides in the United States

What information is found in the 'Statement of Practical Treatment' (first aid) section of a label?

General Standards

Which symptoms point to heat stress rather than pesticide poisoning?

  1. A. Pinpoint pupils, excessive salivation, tearing and muscle twitching that begin shortly after handling an organophosphate insecticide
  2. B. Heavy sweating followed by pale, cool, clammy skin, weakness and nausea that improve with rest, shade and fluids
  3. C. Blurred vision, slurred speech and loss of coordination appearing days after the exposure occurred
  4. D. A skin rash in a covered area that appears two to three days after the application was finished
Correct answer: B. Heavy sweating followed by pale, cool, clammy skin, weakness and nausea that improve with rest, shade and fluids
Heat exhaustion presents with clammy skin, weakness and nausea and responds to rest, shade and fluids; pinpoint pupils and salivation point to cholinesterase-inhibiting insecticide poisoning.
General Standards

Which practice best protects surface water from pesticide runoff?

  1. A. Applying just before a forecast rain so the product is washed into the soil where it is needed
  2. B. Maintaining an untreated vegetative buffer between the treated area and the water, and not applying before heavy rain
  3. C. Treating right up to the water's edge whenever the label lists an aquatic weed as a target pest anywhere in the state or region
  4. D. Mixing and loading the sprayer on the bank of the water body to shorten the distance traveled
Correct answer: B. Maintaining an untreated vegetative buffer between the treated area and the water, and not applying before heavy rain
Buffers of untreated vegetation filter runoff, and applications timed away from heavy rain keep pesticides out of streams, ponds and drains.
