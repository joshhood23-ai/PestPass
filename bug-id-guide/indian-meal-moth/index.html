Indian Meal Moth

Plodia interpunctella — the pantry moth, and the easiest stored-product moth to name: wingspan 5/8 to 3/4 inch, with two-toned wings — pale gray at the base and bronze-copper at the tips. No other pantry moth has that sharp two-color pattern.

How to identify it

  • Size first: wingspan 5/8 to 3/4 inch; at rest the folded wings make a slim moth about 3/8 inch long.
  • The two-tone wing: the inner third of the forewing is pale gray-white; the outer two-thirds are bronze to copper-brown. That clean split is the ID.
  • The larvae: creamy caterpillars about 1/2 inch with a brown head, found in flour, cereal, grain, nuts, dried fruit, spices and dry pet food — trailing silk webbing through the product.
  • Flight: weak, fluttering, zigzag flight, mostly at dusk, around the pantry and kitchen — adults do not feed; the larvae do the damage.
  • Pupation: larvae wander away from the food to spin cocoons in ceiling corners and cabinet edges — which is why moths appear in rooms away from the source.

Signs you have them

  • Silk webbing matting together flour, cereal or grain, with caterpillars inside the package.
  • Cocoons in the corners where walls meet ceilings, and along cabinet edges, away from the food itself.
  • Small two-toned moths fluttering in the pantry at dusk or resting on walls by day.
  • Webbing and larvae in forgotten items: old pet food, bird seed, dried fruit, holiday nuts and spices.

Indianmeal moth larvae leave what evidence in infested food?

  1. Silk webbing throughout the food product and nearby surfaces
  2. Fine white powdery frass like talcum powder
  3. Sandy gritty frass identical to drywood termite pellets in appearance
  4. Hard hexagonal pellets pushed out of kick-out holes
Answer: Silk webbing throughout the food product and nearby surfaces. Indianmeal moth larvae leave distinctive silk webbing throughout infested food — cereals, dried fruit, nuts, pet food. Larvae wander from food to walls/ceilings to pupate.

An Indian meal moth infestation was treated but returned within 2 weeks. The MOST likely reason is:

  1. The treatment happened to be applied at the wrong time of year
  2. An infested product was overlooked during the initial inspection
  3. The insecticide applied was never actually labeled for use against moths
  4. Moth pupae simply migrated in from somewhere outside the structure
Answer: An infested product was overlooked during the initial inspection. Indian meal moth infestations originate from a single infested product — cereal, nuts, dried fruit, bird seed. Missing one infested package is the most common treatment failure cause. All susceptible products must be inspected and discarded or removed.

Pheromone traps used for stored product moths like the Indianmeal moth work by:

  1. Attracting male moths with a synthetic female sex pheromone
  2. Releasing an insecticidal fog that kills any moths on contact
  3. Attracting moths with a food-based bait that they consume and die from
  4. Simply repelling moths away from the monitored area
Answer: Attracting male moths with a synthetic female sex pheromone. Pheromone traps for stored product moths use a synthetic version of the female sex pheromone to lure and capture male moths. Because they attract only males, and only in limited numbers, they function as a monitoring tool to detect presence and track trends — not as a population-reduction method.

Indian Meal Moth questions, answered

How do I identify an Indian meal moth?

By the wings: pale gray at the base and bronze-copper at the tips, with a wingspan of 5/8 to 3/4 inch. The Mediterranean flour moth is larger and uniformly pale gray; no other common pantry moth has the two-tone split.

Where do Indian meal moths come from?

Almost always in with infested food — grain products, nuts, dried fruit, pet food or bird seed bought with eggs or larvae already inside. One infested package seeds the pantry; larvae then wander and pupate in room corners, which spreads the sightings.

What do Indian meal moth larvae eat?

Nearly any dry stored food: flour, cornmeal, cereal, rice, pasta, nuts, seeds, dried fruit, chocolate, spices and dry pet food. They contaminate far more than they eat, webbing product together with silk and frass.

How do you get rid of Indian meal moths?

Find and discard the infested items (check every grain product, pet food and seed), vacuum shelves and corners where cocoons hide, store replacements in sealed hard containers, and use pheromone traps to monitor. Treating the room without removing the source food never works.
