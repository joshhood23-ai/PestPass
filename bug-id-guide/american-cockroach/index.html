American Cockroach

Periplaneta americana — the big sewer-and-basement roach, and the largest cockroach most people will ever see indoors. Adults run 1 1/2 to 2 inches, reddish-brown, with a yellowish-brown margin around the edge of the pronotum. Size alone rules out every small kitchen species.

How to identify it

  • Size first: 1 1/2 to 2 inches — three times a German cockroach. Anything this big is one of the outdoor/peridomestic species.
  • The pronotum edge: a pale yellowish-brown margin rings the shield behind the head; the smokybrown, its closest lookalike, is uniformly dark with no pale edge.
  • Color: glossy reddish-brown all over, with long wings covering the body in both sexes.
  • Where you find it: warm, damp, low places — sewers, basements, boiler rooms, steam tunnels, crawlspaces. It wanders up from drains rather than breeding in cabinets.
  • Nymphs: wingless and reddish-brown; a late nymph is already about an inch long, far bigger than any adult German or brown-banded roach.

Signs you have them

  • Single large roaches appearing near floor drains, basements or utility rooms — usually wanderers from a sewer or crawlspace population.
  • Egg cases (oothecae) glued in dark, protected spots in basements and utility areas — dark reddish-brown, about 3/8 inch.
  • Droppings like coarse coffee grounds or black pepper near pipes and in corners of damp rooms.
  • Clusters around steam pipes, water heaters and other warm harborages in commercial buildings.

American Cockroach questions, answered

How big is an American cockroach?

Adults are 1 1/2 to 2 inches long — the largest of the common house-infesting cockroaches, and about three times the size of a German cockroach.

Is an American cockroach the same as a palmetto bug?

Yes — "palmetto bug" is the regional name used in Florida and the Southeast for the American cockroach (and sometimes its large outdoor cousins, like the smokybrown). It is a nickname, not a different insect.

American cockroach vs smokybrown cockroach — how do I tell them apart?

Look at the pronotum, the shield behind the head. The American has a yellowish-brown margin around its edge; the smokybrown is uniformly dark mahogany-brown with no pale edge — and runs a little smaller, about 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches.

Where do American cockroaches live?

In warm, moist, low places: sewers, storm drains, basements, boiler rooms, crawlspaces and around steam pipes. They breed in those harborages and wander into living spaces — a kitchen cabinet infestation is almost never this species.
