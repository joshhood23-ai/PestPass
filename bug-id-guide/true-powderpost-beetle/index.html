True Powderpost Beetle

Lyctus species — a small (1/8 to 1/4 inch), cylindrical, reddish-brown beetle whose larvae reduce the sapwood of hardwoods to powder. The evidence is unmistakable: pinhead-size round holes in oak, ash or hickory with flour-fine frass sifting out.

How to identify it

  • Size first: adults only 1/8 to 1/4 inch — tiny next to the other wood borers.
  • The holes: perfectly round, 1/32 to 1/16 inch — pinpricks. Deathwatch holes run larger (1/16–1/8 inch); old house borer holes are oval and far bigger.
  • The frass: fine, flour-like powder that pours from holes and piles on surfaces below; it feels like flour or talc between the fingers, never gritty or pelleted.
  • The wood: hardwoods only — this beetle needs the large pores of oak, ash, hickory and similar woods to lay eggs, and attacks the starch-rich sapwood: flooring, furniture, trim, tool handles.
  • The adult: small, narrow, cylindrical, reddish-brown to black, with a distinct head visible from above (its anobiid cousins tuck theirs under a hood-like pronotum).

Signs you have them

  • Pinprick round holes appearing in hardwood flooring, furniture or trim, with fine powder piled beneath.
  • Fresh, light-colored frass that keeps reappearing after cleaning — the mark of an active infestation.
  • Damage confined to the sapwood layer of hardwood items; heartwood and softwoods are left alone.
  • Small reddish-brown beetles on windowsills in spring and summer, near the infested wood.

True Powderpost Beetle questions, answered

How do I know powderpost beetle damage is active?

Fresh frass is the tell: light-colored, flour-fine powder that sifts from holes and re-piles after you clean it up, and bright, sharp-edged new holes. Old, inactive holes are dark inside and produce no new powder.

True powderpost vs old house borer?

Different worlds: true powderpost beetles are 1/8–1/4 inch, make 1/32–1/16 inch round holes in hardwoods, and leave flour-fine frass. The old house borer is 5/8–1 inch, makes large OVAL holes (1/4–3/8 inch) in softwoods like pine, and packs its tunnels with coarse, gritty frass.

What wood do true powderpost beetles attack?

The sapwood of hardwoods — oak, ash, hickory, walnut and similar large-pored woods — because females lay eggs in the open pores and larvae need the starch stored in sapwood. Finished surfaces and varnish can slow, but not always stop, emergence from wood infested before milling.

Are powderpost beetles in furniture a structural risk?

Usually not — lyctids attack hardwood items and flooring, not the softwood framing of houses. The structural wood-borers are the old house borer (softwood) and termites. Correct ID matters because the treatment and urgency are completely different.
