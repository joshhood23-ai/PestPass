Oriental Cockroach

Blatta orientalis — the dark one. Adults are 3/4 to 1 inch, nearly black, glossy and stocky, and they live in cool, damp places — basements, drains, crawlspaces — rather than kitchens. Sometimes called a "waterbug," though it is a true cockroach.

How to identify it

  • Size first: 3/4 to 1 inch — between the small kitchen species and the big American, and much darker than either.
  • Color: dark mahogany-brown to nearly black, with a greasy gloss. No stripes, no bands, no pale pronotum edge.
  • Build: stocky and oval. Females have only short wing pads that leave most of the abdomen bare; males’ wings cover most of the abdomen but they still cannot fly.
  • Where you find it: cool and damp — basements, cellars, floor drains, sewers, under porches, in leaf litter and mulch against the foundation.
  • Season: populations peak outdoors in summer; they move into ground floors and basements, and are slow, clumsy climbers compared with other roaches.

Signs you have them

  • Dark roaches around floor drains, in basements and cellars, or under ground-floor fixtures.
  • Roaches in mulch, leaf litter and debris piled against the foundation, moving in through gaps at grade level.
  • A musty, oily odor in heavy infestations in damp voids.
  • Egg cases — dark, about 3/8 inch — dropped or glued in protected damp spots near the population.

Why does baiting fail as a standalone strategy against a heavy Oriental cockroach infestation coming from a floor drain?

  1. Oriental cockroaches never respond to any control method, chemical or otherwise
  2. Oriental cockroaches are biologically immune to every commercially available bait formulation
  3. Baiting is only effective against cockroach species capable of flight, which excludes Oriental cockroaches
  4. The population depends on the moisture source, so fixing the water problem matters as much as baiting
Answer: The population depends on the moisture source, so fixing the water problem matters as much as baiting. Oriental cockroaches can survive a month without food but only about two weeks without water, so chasing and correcting the moisture source is critical — baiting alone, without addressing the water source, is an incomplete strategy.

Which statement about the Oriental cockroach's flight capability is accurate?

  1. Oriental cockroaches are the strongest fliers of all structural cockroach pests

Oriental Cockroach questions, answered

How big is an Oriental cockroach?

Adults run about 3/4 to 1 inch (males up to about 1 1/4 inches) — bigger than the kitchen species, smaller than an American cockroach, and much darker and stockier than both.

Is an Oriental cockroach a waterbug?

"Waterbug" is a common nickname for this species because it lives around drains and damp basements — but it is a cockroach, not one of the true water bugs (which are aquatic insects). The giveaway is the cockroach body plan: long antennae, shield-like pronotum, and no piercing mouthparts.

Can Oriental cockroaches fly?

No. Females have only short wing pads, and although males have wings covering most of the abdomen, neither sex can fly. They are also poor climbers, which is why they stay at ground level and in basements.

Where do Oriental cockroaches come from?

They breed in cool, damp harborages — sewers, drains, crawlspaces, basements, and the mulch and leaf litter against a foundation — and wander indoors at ground level, especially in summer.
