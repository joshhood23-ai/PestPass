Brown Recluse

Loxosceles reclusa — a plain brown spider, body 1/4 to 3/8 inch, carrying a dark violin-shaped marking on its cephalothorax. The violin suggests; the eyes confirm: a recluse has six eyes in three pairs, where most spiders have eight.

Violin markingA dark violin/fiddle shape on the cephalothorax
Six eyesIn three pairs — the confirmation mark (most spiders have eight)

How to identify it

  • Size first: body 1/4 to 3/8 inch, with legs spanning roughly 1 to 1 1/2 inches — a medium, unremarkable-looking brown spider.
  • The violin: a dark fiddle shape on the cephalothorax (the body section the legs attach to), its neck pointing back toward the abdomen.
  • Six eyes in three pairs — the definitive check under magnification. Many harmless brown spiders wear a violin-ish mark; none of them has this eye pattern.
  • Legs: uniform light brown, no stripes, bands or spines, and not especially long or thick.
  • Where you find it: undisturbed, dry, quiet places — stored boxes, closets, attics, behind furniture, inside rarely worn clothing and shoes. It hunts at night and does not build a prey-catching web; its silk is a messy retreat in its hiding place.

Signs you have them

  • Small, messy silk retreats in stored boxes, closet corners and undisturbed storage — not open webs.
  • Shed skins accumulating in storage areas (a recluse molts several times as it grows).
  • Live spiders in rarely disturbed items: stored clothing, shoes, cardboard boxes, attic insulation edges.
  • Sticky-trap monitors in quiet corners are the standard way infestations are confirmed and measured — sightings of one spider can mean many hidden.

Brown Recluse questions, answered

How can I be sure a spider is a brown recluse?

Two marks together: the dark violin shape on the cephalothorax AND six eyes arranged in three pairs (seen with a hand lens). Many harmless spiders — wolf spiders, house spiders, grass spiders — can look brown and violin-ish, but they have eight eyes and different markings. When in doubt, have the specimen identified.

Brown recluse or wolf spider?

Wolf spiders are bigger, robust and hairy, with bold eye shine and patterned bodies, and they actively hunt in the open. A recluse is smaller, uniformly light brown with fine, smooth legs, the violin mark, six eyes — and it hides. Wolf spiders do not have the violin or the eye pattern.

Do brown recluse bites always cause a wound?

No. Many recluse bites are minor, and most skin lesions blamed on recluse spiders turn out to be something else entirely — infections are far more common. A confirmed diagnosis needs the spider, ideally caught and identified. Serious reactions do occur, so worsening lesions deserve medical care regardless of the suspected cause.

Where are brown recluse spiders found?

The central and south-central US into the Southeast — including parts of Georgia — always indoors or in sheltered spots in undisturbed storage. They are homebodies: a population usually means the spider has been living quietly in the structure for a long time.
