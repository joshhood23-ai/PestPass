Old House Borer

Hylotrupes bajulus — a big, flattened, mottled gray-black longhorn beetle, 5/8 to 1 inch, and the only common wood-boring beetle that leaves oval exit holes. Its larvae spend years tunneling the softwood framing of houses, packing the galleries with coarse frass.

How to identify it

  • Size first: 5/8 to 1 inch — by far the largest of the common house borers; powderpost beetles are a fraction of its size.
  • The holes: distinctly oval, 1/4 to 3/8 inch across. Every powderpost and deathwatch hole is round; carpenter bee holes are round and a full 1/2 inch.
  • The adult: flattened, mottled gray-black longhorn with long antennae and two shiny black bumps on the pronotum behind the head.
  • The wood: softwoods — pine and other conifer framing, especially newer lumber and roof timbers; it does not attack hardwoods.
  • The frass: coarse and gritty, containing small pellets and wood bits, packed tightly inside the tunnels rather than sifting out like powderpost flour.

Signs you have them

  • Large oval holes in exposed pine framing, attic timbers or subfloor joists.
  • Blistered, rippled wood surfaces over shallow, frass-packed tunnels just under the surface.
  • Rasping or ticking sounds from larvae feeding in quiet attics — audible on still days.
  • Big gray-black longhorn beetles emerging indoors in summer from infested framing.

The old house borer has a larval period of approximately:

  1. About 1 year, the same as lyctid powderpost beetles
  2. Around 6 months total, from egg all the way to adult
  3. Roughly 2-10 years inside structural softwood framing
  4. About 4 to 6 weeks total, from egg all the way to adult
Answer: Roughly 2-10 years inside structural softwood framing. The old house borer larval stage commonly lasts 3-5 years and sometimes up to 10+ years — meaning infestations may go undetected for years before exit holes appear.

Which wood-destroying beetle is most associated with re-infesting the SAME piece of wood across multiple beetle generations?

  1. Lyctid powderpost beetles exclusively, among all wood-destroying beetle types
  2. The old house borer (Hylotrupes bajulus), which re-infests the same wood
  3. Carpenter bees, which primarily excavate rather than consume wood
  4. Termites, which technically are not beetles at all
Answer: The old house borer (Hylotrupes bajulus), which re-infests the same wood. Old house borers are notable for their ability to re-infest the same piece of wood across successive generations, allowing damage to continue accumulating in the same structural member over many years without new external infestation sources.

Does bostrichid ("false powderpost beetle") damage typically continue across many generations in the same seasoned wood, the way anobiid or old house borer damage does?

  1. Yes, but only in hardwoods — bostrichids re-infest hardwood while never re-infesting softwood
  2. No — bostrichids cannot complete even one generation in seasoned wood
  3. Generally no — bostrichid activity is usually limited to the seasoning period, like lyctids
  4. Yes, indefinitely — the same multi-generation pattern seen in anobiids and the old house borer
Answer: Generally no — bostrichid activity is usually limited to the seasoning period, like lyctids. Bostrichids share this trait with lyctids: once wood is fully seasoned, ongoing re-infestation is uncommon. This is different from anobiids and the old house borer, both of which are known for persisting across many generations in the same timber.

Old House Borer questions, answered

What makes old house borer holes different?

Shape and size: they are OVAL and large — 1/4 to 3/8 inch across. Powderpost and deathwatch beetles leave small round holes (1/32 to 1/8 inch), and carpenter bees leave perfectly round 1/2-inch holes. Oval means old house borer.

Do old house borers only attack old houses?

Despite the name, no — in Europe it is a pest of newer softwood construction, and in the US it infests pine framing of any age, especially in attics and coastal Southeast homes. The name comes from its habit of reinfesting the same structural wood for years.

How long do old house borer larvae feed?

Years — typically 3 to 5, and sometimes more than a decade in dry wood — which is why damage accumulates quietly in framing before the first oval exit holes appear.

Old house borer or powderpost beetle?

Check the wood and the holes: old house borer = softwood (pine) framing, oval 1/4–3/8 inch holes, coarse gritty frass. True powderpost = hardwood floors and furniture, round 1/32–1/16 inch holes, flour-fine frass. The treatments and the structural stakes are very different.
