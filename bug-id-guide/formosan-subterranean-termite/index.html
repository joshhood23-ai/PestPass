Formosan Subterranean Termite

Coptotermes formosanus — widely considered the most destructive termite in the world. Its colonies run to millions, its soldiers carry oval, teardrop-shaped heads, and its golden-brown swarmers come out at dusk in late spring, drawn to lights.

How to identify it

  • Soldiers: oval, teardrop-shaped amber heads narrowing toward the front, with a fontanelle pore that can exude a milky defensive droplet — the mark that separates them from Eastern subterranean soldiers’ blocky rectangular heads.
  • Swarmers: about 1/2 inch including wings, golden-brown, with fine hairs fringing the wing edges — bigger and paler than the Eastern swarmer.
  • Workers: creamy white, about 1/4 inch — outwardly like other subterranean workers; the soldiers and swarmers carry the ID.
  • Colonies: far larger than native subterraneans — millions of workers foraging up to 300 feet — and able to build moisture-holding carton nests above ground inside walls and roofs, cutting their dependence on the soil.
  • Swarm season: dusk and early evening, late May through early July, swarming to lights.

Signs you have them

  • Massive evening swarms around porch and street lights in late May, June and early July.
  • Carton nests — spongy, soil-and-chewed-wood masses — inside wall voids, attics and roofs, sometimes with no ground connection at all.
  • Damage that advances startlingly fast: hollowed framing, sagging ceilings and floors within a few seasons.
  • Mud tubes like other subterraneans, but walls that stay infested even after soil treatments when an aerial carton nest is present.

How does the Formosan termite soldier's head differ from the Eastern subterranean termite soldier?

  1. Formosan soldiers have a rectangular head and large mandibles; Eastern soldiers have an oval head with a fontanel

The Formosan soldier's oval/teardrop head with a prominent fontanel that exudes white sticky latex is the key field mark separating it from the Eastern subterranean soldier's more rectangular head. Formosan soldiers do have mandibles, and Eastern soldiers have a small fontanel too — head shape is what distinguishes them, not color or the mere presence of a fontanel.

Formosan Subterranean Termite questions, answered

Why is the Formosan termite considered the most destructive?

Colony size and appetite: a mature Formosan colony holds millions of workers foraging up to 300 feet, and it can maintain above-ground carton nests that keep the colony moist without soil contact. It consumes wood far faster than native subterranean species.

Formosan vs Eastern subterranean termite — how do I tell them apart?

Soldiers: Formosan heads are oval and teardrop-shaped with a fontanelle pore; Eastern heads are rectangular and blocky. Swarmers: Formosans are larger (about 1/2 inch), golden-brown with hairy wing edges, and swarm at dusk in late spring; Easterns are smaller, dark, and swarm on spring days.

Can Formosan termites live without contact with the ground?

Yes — that is one of their worst traits. Carton nests built from chewed wood, soil and saliva hold moisture inside walls and roofs, so an aerial colony can persist above ground as long as a moisture source (a roof or plumbing leak) feeds it.

When do Formosan termites swarm?

At dusk and into the early evening, roughly late May through early July, when they are strongly attracted to lights — a porch light covered in golden-brown swarmers at 9 PM in June is the classic picture.
