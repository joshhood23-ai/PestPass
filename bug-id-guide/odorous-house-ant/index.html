Odorous House Ant

Tapinoma sessile — a small 1/8-inch, dark-brown ant with an uneven, humped thorax and a famous party trick: crush one and it smells of rotten coconut. It is one of the most common house-infesting ants in North America.

How to identify it

  • Size first: about 1/8 inch, workers all one size.
  • The smell test: crush a worker between your fingers — a distinct rotten-coconut (some say blue-cheese) odor is diagnostic. No other common house ant does this.
  • Thorax profile: uneven and humped when viewed from the side, unlike the pavement ant’s spined back or the carpenter ant’s smooth arc.
  • Hidden node: the single waist node is flattened and tucked under the front of the abdomen, so the waist looks nearly absent from above.
  • Color: dark brown to nearly black — noticeably darker than the light-brown Argentine ant.

Signs you have them

  • Trails to sweets and pet food, often appearing suddenly in large numbers after rain drives colonies indoors.
  • Nests in wall voids, under flooring, beneath mulch and stones, and in leaf litter against the foundation.
  • Trails climbing trees and shrubs to tend aphids and scale for honeydew.
  • Indoor activity in any season in heated buildings; colonies relocate readily when disturbed.

Odorous House Ant questions, answered

Why is it called the odorous house ant?

Crushed workers release a chemical that smells like rotten coconut — the easiest field ID for this species. The odor comes from the same family of compounds found in coconut and blue cheese.

Odorous house ant vs pavement ant — how do I tell them apart?

Both are about 1/8 inch and brown. The pavement ant has small spines on its thorax and a two-segmented waist, and nests under slabs; the odorous house ant has a humped, spineless thorax, a hidden one-segment waist, and the coconut smell when crushed.

Are odorous house ants harmful?

They do not sting or damage wood. The problem is numbers: colonies hold multiple queens, nest almost anywhere, and invade kitchens in force — a persistent nuisance and food-contamination pest.

Where do odorous house ants nest indoors?

In wall voids, under floors and insulation, around plumbing and water heaters — anywhere warm and close to moisture — with outdoor colonies in mulch, soil and debris feeding workers inside.
