Eastern Subterranean Termite

Reticulitermes flavipes — the termite behind most structural infestations in the eastern US. Workers are creamy white and soft-bodied, about 1/4 inch; soldiers carry a rectangular, blocky amber head. The colony lives in the soil and reaches wood through mud tubes.

How to identify it

  • Workers: creamy white, soft, about 1/4 inch, with small heads — the pale insects that spill out of damaged wood.
  • Soldiers: a rectangular, square-sided amber head capsule with dark, curved mandibles — that blocky head is the species’ signature.
  • Swarmers (alates): dark-bodied, about 3/8 inch including two pairs of equal-length wings; straight, bead-like antennae and no pinched waist — the marks that separate them from flying ants.
  • Where it lives: underground colonies that can take years to mature; workers commute into structures through mud tubes and moisture-damaged wood.
  • Swarm season: warm spring days, typically February through May in Georgia, often right after rain.

Signs you have them

  • Pencil-width mud tubes running up foundation walls, piers and into framing — the colony’s highway from soil to wood.
  • Discarded wings and dead swarmers on windowsills after a spring swarm.
  • Wood that sounds hollow or papery when tapped, with soil packed into the galleries.
  • Blistered or bubbling paint and sagging floors over damaged framing.

Eastern Subterranean Termite questions, answered

How do I tell a termite swarmer from a flying ant?

Termite swarmers have straight, bead-like antennae, a broad waist with no pinch, and two pairs of equal-length wings. Flying ants have elbowed antennae, a pinched one- or two-node waist, and front wings clearly longer than the hind wings.

Eastern subterranean vs Formosan termite — what is the difference?

Soldier heads separate them: Eastern soldiers have rectangular, blocky heads; Formosan soldiers have oval, teardrop-shaped heads with a fontanelle pore. Formosan swarmers are also larger (about 1/2 inch), golden-brown, and swarm at dusk in late spring; Eastern swarmers are smaller, darker, and swarm on spring days.

When do eastern subterranean termites swarm?

On warm spring days — roughly February to May in Georgia — frequently just after rain. A daytime indoor swarm in spring is this species until proven otherwise.

Why do subterranean termites build mud tubes?

Workers dry out quickly in open air, so they travel inside earthen tubes that hold soil moisture and hide them from predators. Tubes on a foundation are how a ground colony reaches the wood of a house.
