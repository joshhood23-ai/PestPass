Pavement Ant

Tetramorium species — a dark brown-black, 1/8-inch ant with a two-segmented waist and small spines on its thorax. It nests under sidewalks, driveways and slabs, pushing up little mounds of soil between the cracks — hence the name.

How to identify it

  • Size first: about 1/8 inch, workers all one size.
  • Thorax spines: a pair of small spines points back from the rear of the thorax — under magnification this separates it from every other small brown house ant.
  • Two-node waist: two distinct segments (petiole and postpetiole), where the odorous house ant’s single node hides under its abdomen.
  • Texture: the head and thorax carry fine lengthwise grooves; the body looks dull, not shiny.
  • Where you find it: nesting in soil under pavement, slabs and stones, foraging up through expansion joints and foundation cracks into ground floors.

Signs you have them

  • Small cone-shaped mounds of excavated soil along sidewalk, driveway and patio cracks in spring and summer.
  • Slow-moving trails along foundation edges and through cracks at floor level.
  • Winged swarmers emerging indoors in winter and early spring from colonies under heated slabs.
  • Territorial sidewalk battles in spring — two colonies fighting in a writhing mass on the pavement is a classic pavement ant sight.

Which ant species is small, dark brown to black, and commonly nests in the soil beneath sidewalks, driveways, and slab foundations?

  1. Fire ant
  2. Pharaoh ant
  3. Carpenter ant

Answer: Pavement ant. Pavement ants are small, dark ants that characteristically nest under slabs, sidewalks, and driveways, often leaving small piles of excavated soil. Scientific name: Tetramorium immigrans (the North American pavement ant, formerly grouped under T. caespitum).

Pavement Ant questions, answered

How do I identify a pavement ant?

A dark brown to black ant about 1/8 inch long with two waist segments and a pair of small spines on the thorax (visible with a hand lens), plus fine grooves on the head and thorax. The soil mounds it pushes up between pavement cracks are the other classic sign.

Pavement ant vs odorous house ant?

Pavement ants have thorax spines and two waist segments, and no odor. Odorous house ants are spineless with a humped thorax and a single hidden waist segment — and smell like rotten coconut when crushed.

Do pavement ants bite or sting?

They can sting in theory but almost never do — stings to people are rare and mild. They are a nuisance and food-contamination pest, not a dangerous one.

Why are there ants coming up through my floor?

Colonies nesting under the slab forage through expansion joints, plumbing penetrations and foundation cracks. Winter indoor swarmers usually mean the colony is under a heated slab inside the building footprint.
