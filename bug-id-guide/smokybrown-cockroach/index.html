Smokybrown Cockroach

Periplaneta fuliginosa — the American cockroach’s darker twin. Adults are 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches, uniformly dark mahogany-brown all over with full-length wings — and no pale edge on the pronotum. It breeds outdoors and in attics across the Southeast and flies to lights on warm nights.

How to identify it

  • Size first: 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches — a big roach, slightly smaller than the American.
  • Uniform color: dark mahogany-brown over the whole body, wings included. If the pronotum has a pale yellowish margin, it is an American cockroach instead.
  • Wings: full-length in both sexes, and adults are strong fliers — they come to porch lights and slip in around rooflines and attic vents.
  • Where you find it: tree holes, mulch, woodpiles, gutters and attics. Indoors it is usually a wanderer from the attic or the yard, not a breeding kitchen pest.
  • Nymphs: wingless, uniformly dark mahogany-brown; Oriental nymphs are similar in color but more oval and compact.

Signs you have them

  • Large dark roaches in attics, garages and upper floors — this species works from the roofline down, not the sewer up.
  • Adults flying to outdoor lights on warm, humid nights in late spring and summer.
  • Populations in tree holes, mulch beds, woodpiles and leaf-filled gutters right against the house.
  • Egg cases in attic insulation, soffits and stored cardboard in garages.

  2. American cockroach nymph — uniformly reddish-brown, no white tips

Answer: Smokybrown cockroach nymph — white-tipped antennae and cerci. Smokybrown nymphs are dark mahogany-brown with conspicuous white tips on the antennae and cerci — the fastest way to separate them from American nymphs, which are larger, more reddish-brown, and lack the white tips. Both are peridomestic species that enter attics.

Smokybrown Cockroach questions, answered

Smokybrown vs American cockroach — what is the difference?

Check the pronotum, the shield behind the head: the American has a yellowish-brown margin around its edge, while the smokybrown is uniformly dark mahogany-brown with no pale edge. The smokybrown also runs a little smaller — about 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches against 1 1/2 to 2.

Can smokybrown cockroaches fly?

Yes — adults have full-length wings and are capable fliers. On warm nights they fly to lights, which is one way they reach attics, soffits and upper-story windows.

Where do smokybrown cockroaches live?

Outdoors and in the attic zone: tree holes, mulch, woodpiles, gutters, soffits and attics. They need more moisture than the indoor species and rarely establish breeding populations in living spaces.

Are smokybrowns common in Georgia and Florida?

Yes — they are one of the dominant large outdoor cockroaches across the Gulf Coast and Southeast, including Georgia and Florida, where warmth and humidity let them breed year-round around structures.
