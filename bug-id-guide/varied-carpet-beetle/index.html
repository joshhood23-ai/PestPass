Varied Carpet Beetle

Anthrenus verbasci — a tiny round beetle, about 1/8 inch, wearing a mottled coat of white, brown and orange-yellow scales. The adult is harmless window dressing; the damage is done by its hairy, banded larvae eating wool, fur and feathers in quiet corners.

How to identify it

  • Size first: about 1/8 inch, round-oval and compact — ladybug-shaped but far smaller and muted.
  • The mottling: irregular patches of white, brown and orange-yellow scales cover the wing covers. The black carpet beetle is solid dark; drugstore and cigarette beetles are plain uniform brown.
  • The larva: a fuzzy, banded "woolly bear" about 1/8 to 1/4 inch with alternating light and dark bands and tufts of bristles — this is the stage that eats carpets, clothing and upholstery.
  • Adults: found on windowsills and flowers, feeding on pollen — a windowsill beetle in spring is often the first sign of larvae indoors.
  • Where the larvae hide: under baseboards, in air ducts, closets and drawers — wherever lint, hair, feathers and dead insects collect undisturbed.

Signs you have them

  • Irregular holes in wool clothing, carpets, upholstery and stored natural-fiber items (synthetics are ignored).
  • Shed larval skins and bristly larvae in closets, drawers, ducts and under furniture.
  • Tiny mottled beetles gathering on sunny windowsills in spring.
  • Damage concentrated in undisturbed storage — the back of closets, under heavy furniture, inside pianos and vents.

Fabric pests (clothes moths, carpet beetles) primarily damage textiles containing:

  1. Keratin, a protein found in wool, fur, feathers, and hair

  3. Pure synthetic fibers like polyester, with no natural fiber blend
  4. Cotton exclusively, with no other fiber type present
Answer: Keratin, a protein found in wool, fur, feathers, and hair. Fabric pest larvae feed on keratin, a protein found in wool, fur, feathers, and hair — not on synthetic fibers. (Silk is fibroin and leather is collagen, not keratin, though larvae may still damage soiled silk and leather.) A pure polyester or nylon item is not a food source unless it is blended with natural fiber or heavily soiled with food or body oils that attract feeding.

Varied Carpet Beetle questions, answered

How do I identify a varied carpet beetle?

Adults: a round 1/8-inch beetle mottled with white, brown and orange-yellow scales. Larvae: fuzzy, banded "woolly bears" with bristle tufts. Uniformly brown 1/8-inch beetles are drugstore or cigarette beetles; a solid dark one is the black carpet beetle.

Carpet beetle larva or bed bug?

The classic confusion — and easy to settle: carpet beetle larvae are hairy and tapered with visible bands and bristles; bed bugs are smooth, flat, oval and hairless. Hairy means carpet beetle larva, never a bed bug.

What do carpet beetle larvae eat?

Animal materials: wool, fur, feathers, hair, silk, leather and the dead insects that collect in ducts and window tracks. They graze lint and pet hair under baseboards and furniture, then move on to woolens and upholstery. Cotton and synthetics are not food.

Why are carpet beetles on my windowsill?

Adults feed on pollen outdoors and are drawn to light; they collect at windows trying to get out (or in). A spring windowsill gathering is a sign to check closets, ducts and stored woolens for the larvae doing the real damage.
